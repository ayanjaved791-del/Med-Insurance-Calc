Method

  1. Removed 1 duplicate row, leaving 1,337 records with no missing values
  2. Encoded gender and smoking as 0/1, and region as yes/no columns
  3. Added features: age², obesity (BMI ≥ 30), smoker × obese, smoker × BMI
  4. Split the data 80/20 for training and testing (random_state = 42)
  5. Trained a multiple linear regression and estimated likely ranges with 5-fold cross-validation

Limitations

  • Trained on one public US dataset, so this is an educational estimate, not a quote
  • About 1 in 12 people pay much more than expected, for reasons not in the data (e.g. existing conditions)
  • Ages outside 18–64 and extreme BMIs are guesses beyond the data
  • Real premiums depend on plan type, deductible, employer and local rules

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is this a real insurance quote?

No. It's an educational estimate from a statistical model trained on a public US dataset. Use it to understand cost drivers and compare scenarios, then get real quotes from insurers.

Is my data stored anywhere?

No. The model runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you enter is sent to a server. Saved scenarios are kept only on your device.

Why does smoking matter so much?

In the data, smokers pay about 3.8× more than non-smokers on average. For smokers with a BMI of 30 or more, the extra cost roughly doubles again. The enhanced model captures this combination.

What does "likely range" mean?

It's where most people with a similar profile landed, based on how far off the model's cross-validated predictions were. A small share of people pay much more for reasons not in the data.

What's the difference between the two models?

The original is a plain linear regression (R² 0.81). The enhanced model adds age², an obesity flag and smoker interactions, which lifts R² to 0.91 and cuts the average error from $4,177 to $2,277.
